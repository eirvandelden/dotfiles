# Writing as Etienne

Use this guide when drafting messages on Etienne's behalf. Etienne confirmed these preferences on 16 October 2026.

Drafting does not authorise sending, posting or changing anything in another application. Draft in chat and ask for explicit authorisation before publishing (playbook rule 6).

## Core preferences

- Begin with a friendly greeting. Etienne often adds an emoji to the greeting: "Hi Thalion 👋," or "Hoi hoi 👋".
- Give context before the question or request. Explain why you are writing and what led to the message. Do not remove the introduction merely to make the message shorter.
- Keep the tone warm, natural and practical. Use emojis naturally; do not restrict warmth to a single closing smiley. A waving emoji in the greeting and a smiley later suited a message to a colleague.
- Use concrete references, such as a relevant link, example or tool, when they help the recipient.
- Clearly state what remains unfinished. Preserve uncertainty and distinguish completed work from drafts or plans.

## Suggested message flow

1. Friendly greeting, often with an emoji.
2. Context: what prompted the message and why it matters.
3. Concrete information or references, including unfinished work where relevant.
4. A clear question or next step.

This is a flexible pattern, not a mandatory template. Match the audience, language and situation. Do not force identical greetings or a fixed emoji count into every message.

## Illustrative example

This fictional example shows the structure. It is not a factual message to send.

> Hi Thalion 👋,
>
> Following our conversation, I thought it would help to gather a few things before we talk. We had similar questions in our team.
>
> We have a draft ready, but we still need to verify it.
>
> Do you have an example we could use? That would give us something concrete to compare 🙂

## Limits

- Apply this guide to writing on Etienne's behalf. Do not imitate his style in your own explanations to him.
- The core preferences are confirmed. The flow and example illustrate them; they are not a complete profile of his writing.
- Use relevant examples of his writing when available. Follow his current instructions if they differ from this guide.
- Never invent facts, commitments or completed work to make a draft sound natural.
